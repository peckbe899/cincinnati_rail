## area-net (review)

The area network's code holds up. Channel order is RGB everywhere (unet.py:54, blocks_noguide.py:39, edgenet.py:51), and scaling is /255 on both the training and prediction side. The street:block weight of 3:1 plus the edge-weight map balances the loss well: streets carry 43-58% of the loss on each plate. The 0.5 threshold is close to the best one (plate 44: accuracy 0.9147 at 0.40 against 0.9126 at 0.50). Tiling covers plates whose size is not a multiple of the step, and the third output is masked correctly. A CPU rerun of area_ng1 reproduces the stored DirectML tiles to within 1/255, so the backend and the stored maps can be trusted. Paper colour on unplaced plates (L 201-218) is inside the range the colour jitter covers. Labels taken from network outlines are negligible (1-3 sides per plate, from the N_meta.json files). The third (edge) output is not read by any later step. Where the results are hurt is the labels and the measuring, not a coding bug. Today's parcels label railway ground as block; that ground holds 29% of all errors on plate 44, and leaving it out lifts accuracy from 0.913 to 0.935. Scored against Ben's checked street map of 44, area_ng1 (blank guide) puts its edge within 3 px (half size) of 64.7% of his line length, against 45.3% for the stored edge_v1 prediction. So the '48% blank vs 95% guided' comparison overstates the gap, but edge_v1's stored map was made from older, unrefined labels, so the reason for the gap is not settled. GroupNorm statistics are taken per tile, so the answer depends on how the plate is cut into tiles. At tile seams the predicted block/street call flips 5 times as often as elsewhere, and 1.3-2.3% of pixels change when the tiling changes. Blending overlapping tiles with a Hann window removes the seams and gains 0.2 point. Other problems are smaller: the line network learns from area predictions made on the area network's own training plates, the four training plates cover a narrow range of map types, and the last weights are saved instead of the best or averaged ones. ### area-3: GroupNorm uses each tile's own statistics, so answers depend on the tiling and seams appear

- severity medium, after check low; verdict **partly**; effort small; priority None
- where: C:/Users/peckb/Documents/_Personal/_Cincinnati Rail/3D/tools/sanborn/unet/unet.py:75
- **claim:** GroupNorm (line 35) normalises over the whole tile. A pixel's answer therefore depends on map content far beyond the network's 200 px conv reach and on where the tile boundaries fall. predict_full (lines 75-89) pastes hard-cut 640 px tile centres side by side, so the answer jumps along tile seams. Training uses 384 px crops; prediction uses 768 px tiles, and 1536 px tiles are worse again.
- **evidence:** Stored *_pred_area_ng1.png, 29 plates: block/street flips across seam lines run at 0.0357 per px on seam columns against 0.0067 elsewhere, and |dP| is 5.6x higher across seam rows. CPU test on plate 44: replacing content at least 136 px away (beyond the 100 px conv radius) with plain paper flips 0.7-3.1% of the centre's answers. With pad 192 (768/192) seams remain (0.0166 vs 0.0068). Between tiling schemes 1.3-2.3% of pixels change answer. Accuracy on rows 640-2560, cols 1280-3840: 0.9223 as run (768/64), 0.9258 with 768/192, 0.9132 with 1536/128, 0.9245 with Hann blending at step 384. Blending removes the seam excess (0.0029-0.0047 vs 0.0068 baseline).
- **impact:** Straight, axis-parallel false edges along seams. They are only 0.6% of all predicted edge pixels, but straight-side fitting in outlines.py can latch onto them. About 1-2% of every plate's answer is tiling noise, a sizeable share of the 8.7% error rate.
- **fix:** In predict_full, use overlapping 768 tiles at step 384, weighted by a 2-D Hann window, and normalise by the summed weights. This is about 2.8x the compute, roughly 10 s per plate on the GPU. Keep the tile size near the training crop size, or train on 512-768 crops. Longer term, use a normalisation that does not depend on the tile's content, such as BatchNorm with stored running statistics; the earlier DirectML BatchNorm trouble may have come from the train/eval switch, not the backend. Test that on CPU first.
- **check, reason:** The mechanism is real. GroupNorm(8,o) (unet.py:35) normalises over the whole tile. Changing content beyond the 100 px conv radius flips 1.1-2.4% of centre answers in my test. predict_full hard-pastes tile centres, and the flip rate on seam columns is about 5 times the rate elsewhere. The impact is overstated, though. On the whole plate, removing seams with pad 192 changes 1.7% of pixels but gains only 0.14 accuracy points and nothing against Ben's lines, so the changed pixels are mostly noise flipping both ways, not 'a sizeable share of the 8.7% error'. In sub-regions the effect swings: pad 192 gained 1.0-2.0 points in two of my regions; Hann blending gained 1.25 in one and lost 0.13 in the other. The more consistent lever is pad: 64 px of discard is less than the network's 100 px conv radius (RF 200). Pad 192 costs the same as Hann at step 384 and did at least as well in every region tested. 'Keep the tile near the training crop size' is not supported: 384/64 gave the same accuracy as 768/64 (0.8954 vs 0.8960) while changing 3.6% of answers.
- **check, corrected_claim:** Per-tile GroupNorm and hard-pasted tiles make about 1.5-3.5% of answers depend on the tiling and give a 5x flip rate along seams. The net accuracy effect on plate 44 is small, +0.14 points with pad 192 and no change against Ben's lines. The cheap fix is a pad at or above the 100 px conv radius (for example 768/192, step 384), optionally with Hann blending of the kept centres. Smaller tiles do not help.
- **check, evidence:** v4_seams.py over 45 stored *_pred_area_ng1.png: 0.5-flip rate 0.0326 on seam columns vs 0.0062 mid-tile, rows 0.0343 vs 0.0071. Plate 44: boundary px within 2 px of a seam = 1.42% of boundary px (the seam band is 1.15% of the plate). v5_cpu.py (CPU): ring beyond 136 px set to paper -> centre flips 0.0242/0.0107/0.0237. v6_blend.py regions 1920:3200,2560:4480 and 0:1280,3200:5120, accuracy 768/64 vs 768/192 vs Hann: 0.8718/0.8918/0.8843 and 0.8720/0.8819/0.8707. Hann removes the seam excess (0.023 -> 0.0047). v11_fullpad.py whole plate 44: 768/64 0.9126 / iou_street 0.7014; 768/192 0.9140 / 0.7045; 1.73% of pixels differ. v12: Ben lines within 3 px 0.626 vs 0.626.

### critic-3: No torch seed: retraining is not reproducible, so single-run design comparisons cannot be separated from run-to-run noise

- severity low, after check None; verdict **None**; effort small; priority None
- where: tools/sanborn/unet/unet.py:105
- **claim:** Both training loops seed only the numpy crop sampler (np.random.default_rng(0)). The network's initial weights come from torch's unseeded RNG, and DirectML kernels are not guaranteed deterministic. Two trainings on identical data therefore give different networks and different plate-44 scores. Every design choice so far (lines_v1 to v2, guided_v3 to edge_v1, guided vs blank, and any future retrain after Ben's checks) rests on one run per design, scored on one plate.
- **evidence:** unet.py:105 'rng = np.random.default_rng(0)' and unet.py:112 'net = UNet().to(dev)'; edgenet.py:85 and :89 are the same. A search of tools/sanborn for manual_seed finds nothing. The size of the noise is unmeasured (no GPU run was allowed), but the stored history shows edge_v1's held-out accuracy moving 0.935-0.982 between checkpoints of a single run (research-training finding 5), so differences of a few points between runs are plausible.
- **impact:** An apparent gain or loss of 1-5 points between two models on plate 44 may be seed noise, so a retrain after Ben's next checked plates could look better or worse for no real reason.
