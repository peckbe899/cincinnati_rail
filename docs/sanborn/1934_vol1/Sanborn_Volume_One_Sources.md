# Sanborn coverage of the lost West End: what exists, what you hold, what to get

## 1. Which volume covers what

The whole clearance area south of West Liberty Street is in **Volume One**, which the Library of Congress (LOC) has not put online in any state.

- **Vol. 1 / Vol. 3 line: centre of West Liberty Street** (verified). The Vol. 3 index lists W. Liberty in even numbers only (2 to 1244, sheets 210 down to 203), each marked as one side of the street.
- **Vol. 1 / Vol. 2 line: centre of Vine Street**, river to Liberty (verified). The Vol. 2 index lists Vine in even numbers only.
- **Vol. 3 west edge** is lettered "Volume Five". Whether the rail yard west of McLean and south of about Liberty is in Vol. 1 or Vol. 3 is not settled.

| Area | Volume | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Kenyon-Barr (297 acres, lower West End) | Vol. 1, effectively all | Likely: volume lines verified, project edges recalled |
| Laurel Homes (Liberty, Lincoln Park Drive, John, Linn) | Vol. 1 | Verified from the HABS record |
| Lincoln Court | Vol. 1 | Likely: bounding streets unconfirmed |
| Dayton Street area, Findlay to Bank | Vol. 3, sheets 213 to 217, plus 218 east of Central | Verified |
| Liberty north frontage up to Findlay | Vol. 3, sheets 203 to 210 | Verified |
| I-75 strip north of Liberty | Vol. 3: 203/204, 213/214, 234/235 reasonably safe | Alignment recalled; Camp Washington sheets doubtful |

## 2. What is online

**Online and public domain** (LOC's own rights statement, verified):

- Vol. 3, 1904 base corrected to April 1950, LOC item sanborn06645_026, sheets 201 to 305. Last correction is revision 33, dated 4/50.
- Vol. 2, corrected to 4-50, item 025, sheets 101 to 191. It holds none of the West End.
- URL pattern (swap the sheet number): `https://tile.loc.gov/image-services/iiif/service:gmd:gmd408m:g4084m:g4084cm:g4084cm_g06645195003:06645_03_1950-0206/full/pct:25/0/default.jpg`

**Not online** (verified from the catalogue records):

- Vol. 1, 1934, 86 sheets (item 018).
- Vol. 1, 1934 corrected to April 1950, 88 sheets (item 024).

The reason is unknown. A copyright renewal is one hypothesis, but the 1934 Vol. 2 is online, so it is unproven.

## 3. What you already hold

- **1904 Vol. 1, all 83 sheets, corrected to about 1930-31** (verified). These are the colour Cincinnati library scans at 50 ft to the inch, and they cover the whole West End south of Liberty. This is your newest state for the area.
- **1922 "Atlas of Cincinnati"**: the Drive PDFs "Sanborn Cincinnati 4" to "95" (three opened, all this atlas). It is at 100 or 200 ft to the inch with simplified footprints and no corrections. The West End is probably on plates 1 and 3 to 9; plates 1 to 3 may be missing from Drive.
- **No 1934 or 1950 Vol. 1** was found in Drive, the repo log or the build manifest. That is absence of evidence only.

So your sheets stop 20 to 30 years before the aerials, and predate Laurel Homes and Lincoln Court.

## 4. Ways to get Volume One, ranked

1. **Cincinnati History Library and Archives (Museum Center).** Reported in 2021 to hold print volumes with pasted updates. This is the best chance of a state later than 1950, and in colour. It costs an email, then a visit or a scan request. Which volumes and dates it holds is unknown.
2. **Ohio Web Library Sanborn collection** (ohioweblibrary.org/sanborn). Free to Ohio residents and reported to take a public library card login. It is black and white from microfilm. It probably holds "1904-June 1950 vol. 1, 1934-Apr. 1950", but only Vol. 10 is confirmed in that state.
3. **Kent State University Map Library.** It holds LOC's duplicate Ohio set and scanned Cincinnati in colour in its first phase. Its Vol. 1 may be the uncorrected 1934 state, or held back for copyright.
4. **LOC Duplication Services.** Scans cost 50 USD each, plus 10 USD per foot oversize and a 25 to 75 USD process fee. For 88 sheets, 4,400 USD is a floor, and LOC may refuse. A free Ask a Librarian question on why Vol. 1 is not online comes first.
5. **ProQuest Digital Sanborn Maps** through a subscribing library: black and white, same source as option 2.

**Rights:** if the 1934 volume was renewed, it is in copyright until 1 January 2030; if not, it is public domain. Nobody has checked the renewal. Tracing footprints is low risk either way, but republishing sheet images is not safe until the check is done.

## 5. The online Vol. 3 sheets: uses and limits

**Good for:**

- Tracing the Dayton Street area and the northern West End.
- Scans are 300 dpi colour, about 5 cm per pixel (measured on sheet 206).
- Sheet 206 is the same base sheet as 1904, so your 1904 control points should transfer nearly unchanged.
- Storeys, material colour and roof symbol are on essentially every building.
- 1950 content is confirmed, for example Crosley Field, Clopay and Central Parkway.

**Limits:**

- The sheets stop at Liberty and show nothing of Kenyon-Barr.
- **Heights in feet are not printed on ordinary dwellings or flats.** They appear only on some stores, garages, churches and factories, so use storey counts for houses.
- Use letters and house numbers are on street-front buildings only.
- Slips are hand-pasted, so expect offsets of a foot or two.
- The state ends at April 1950, nine years before clearance.
- Red circled numbers are probably Census block numbers; do not trace them.
