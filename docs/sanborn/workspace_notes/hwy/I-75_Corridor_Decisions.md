# Cincinnati Interstate Corridors: Road Types by KMZ Piece (working, not yet in the fact tracker)

Ruled 18 and 19 October 2026 on Final_Cincinnati_Highways.kmz. Kilometres are within each KMZ piece, as drawn. The I-75 split is on the stitched I-75 line (66.16 km from I-275 at Sharonville). Map: Cincinnati Interstate Types artifact; KMZ: Cincinnati_Interstate_Corridors.kmz.

## Rules

- Types follow land use (Metro and TRI nodes, hillsides, river edges), not the Middle Ring. The Middle Ring stays the toll boundary for I-75 and I-71.

- The Middle Ring is the Périph/A86 analog and the I-275 Loop the Francilienne/A86 analog: further out than Paris, cutting through existing towns, built to suit.

- APEx always takes the centre when it shares a right-of-way with an Interstate. Wherever APEx and TRI share, it is quad track at least; where APEx traffic could double (interlining), the median is kept wide enough for more APEx tracks.

- Compact interchanges on the Japanese model wherever ramps or overpasses are needed; frontage-to-frontage links (the I-75/Middle Ring spur) are outside that rule.

- The Storrow-analog has truck clearance; tolls on I-75 and I-71 keep trucks off, so on the toll-free rings Storrow stretches carry trucks.

- Hazmat: each side keeps a tunnel-free river crossing (west on the outer loop's bridge, east on Combs-Hehl).

## Lane-level junctions (19 October)

Ben, 19 October: roads are drawn as their lanes, not as roads, and every lane connects: lanes end where their exit ramp starts and begin where an entry ramp ends, with merges at every junction. Lane drop and lane add at junctions.

- Centrelines: the KMZ lines are polylines with kinks at their vertices and a few 1 to 2.5 m backtracks at piece joins. Positions are smoothed along chainage (Gaussian, 25 m), with chainage kept as the parameter, so every km reference still points at the same place. The smoothed line stays within 5 m of the KMZ, mostly within 1.5 m. Lanes are drawn at fixed distances from it, and changes in lane offset ease in over about 100 m.

- Lanes keep their own line to the end; each ramp is fitted to the lanes it takes or gives (its end moves onto them, the shift fading out over 150 m along the ramp; at most 2.6 m).

- Ramps are drawn as their lanes too. Each is a smooth curve through its design points, and a ramp that splits from 2 lanes and joins with 1 narrows midway.

- Ramp lanes (Ben): 1 lane by default; 2 where a road ends and splits. A 3-lane road splitting gives 2 lanes each, [left][left or right][right]. A 2-lane road splitting gives 1 each, or 2+1 where traffic calls for it. A 4-lane road gives 2+2.

- Exits on a road that carries on: the exit takes the outer lane. Where only 2 lanes arrive, the outer lane is an option lane (through or exit), so the road keeps both. Two exits to the two directions of the other road share that one lane. It widens into two lanes side by side over a 200 m split zone before the two ramps part.

- Entries: two entries from the other road arrive side by side and merge over a 200 m merging zone (Ben, 19 October: no point merges), then join the road as one added lane. Where the road widens there, they add one lane each. A lane the road does not carry on runs 300 m as an acceleration lane and tapers into the next lane.

- A road that ends at a junction (the spurs, the truck bypass, the inner ring north at J7 and J8) and the forks (J5a, J5b, J14) hand all their lanes to the ramps. Each ramp leaves on its own lanes, with no swing across the carriageway.

- Town sections: the side lane is the road's own outer lane (the ring's third lane, or an added lane on the two-lane radials). A town section therefore carries 2 sunken lanes plus the side lane, and the drawing shows it as the third lane beside the sunken two (Ben: a 3-lane road stays 3 lanes up to the split).

- M2 (Ben): the spur splits 1+1, so the loop stays at 3 lanes.

- M3 and M4 (Ben: auxiliary lane): the 2-lane spur ramp adds both lanes, and the fourth lane runs on to the next exit.
  - M4: the next exit is the I-75 complex at Florence. The fourth lane runs 17.6 km west on the loop and becomes the exit lane to I-75.
  - M3: the fourth lane has no room in a tunnel or a town section, so it ends after 5.0 km, just before the Middle Ring tunnel (km 13.2 to 14.2 on the Middle Ring piece). It never reaches J5a.

- M1 (my change): the spur splits 1+1, not 2+1. The Milford town section begins 270 m from the junction, and a 2-lane ramp would put 4 lanes into a road that carries 3 there.

- J15 and F15 are one complex. The loop leaves I-71 90 m west of the I-75 crossing, so the I-75 ramps on the west side could not join the shared road before it splits. The shared road now splits just short of I-75 and each branch passes under it. The I-75 ramps on the west side tie straight into I-71 and the loop beyond the split: 18 ramps.
