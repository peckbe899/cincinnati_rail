CUT approaches

Arch viaduct (Ben): the six APEx tracks run a set height (6.5 m) above the deck cross streets from Marshall to W 6th, so every street passes under. CUT's upper platforms rise to about 160.3 m. The four lower-level tracks ride the viaduct from Ludlow to Bank, then dive between Bank and Findlay and pass under Findlay into the deck, about 550 m from the lower platforms. Bank is lowered and Findlay raised 2 m to keep both open; Dayton, Exeter and York end at the dive.

North trunk (14 October, Ben: every path on one viaduct, Ludlow to CUT): west to east, Transvalley/R5, APEx Dayton/Norwood (4), APEx Indianapolis (2), TRI B/D and ORR/intercity E-H. Since 22 October the rails stand 162.5 m over Ludlow, which stays at ground on its 1914 line, and climb 0.28% to 166.6 m at the deck tip (they had reached the ground at Ludlow, 155.5 m, under the 1922 viaduct), then hold 6.5 m over the lobe streets. Hopple comes down to deck level and passes under. TRI B/D ride the viaduct from Ludlow: Arlington (B) on the east side at 165.9 m (164.6 m before 22 October), centred on the KMZ point; D leaves B north of Camp Washington by a flyover (southbound D on the west of B, northbound D on the east, crossing over B as it climbs); Camp Washington (B, D) on the viaduct at about 165.2 m; then 3.3% down south of it into a box under the deck to Straight (151.6 m), bored from Straight. Peak service on the shared pair: B every 4 min, D every 8 min (22.5 trains an hour each way). The Indianapolis pair and D climb 2.5% on one deck from s 3,644 to 174.3 m and turn west on the KMZ curve over the Dayton/Norwood and Transvalley/R5 tracks, then over RER A (167.0 m) as ruled. R5 leaves the west pair for the Cumminsville Spur (its levels west of the trunk are not set). ORR E moves 22 m east from Camp Washington to Arlington to make room for B/D. At the dive Garrard line is raised 1.8 m onto the box roof, Rawson lowered 1.2 m and Township 2.8 m to pass under the ramp; Meeker and New street N end at it. North of Ludlow the at-grade layout is not set and B is drawn on its KMZ line.

All eight lower platforms are 305 m, centred under the APEx field. The platform field is the one on the architects’ plot plan of 1 June 1931: straight parallel tracks, 3.97 m between the tracks of a pair, 11.68 m across an island (8.3 m platforms), seven concourse islands carrying APEx 1-14 from the building westward, and platform M beside the building: the 1933 mail platform, now CVG Express 15-16 (Ben). The plan’s westmost through track is the freight passing track. The platforms keep the ruled length (the plan is cut off at its north end); track 16 and platform M run the full length (Ben). The eight lower platforms (A-H) are centred under the APEx platforms, from platform 7’s west edge to platform M’s east edge. The west headhouse has moved 30 m east to the freight passing track, and the west park runs on to it. South of the platforms six APEx tracks (Kentucky 4, Cardinal 2) follow Ben’s General CUT Path KMZ: straight south, one curve of about 870 m radius between W 8th and W 6th, then the four Kentucky tracks cross the Ohio on their own bridge (bearing 204 degrees), 98 m east of the CS bridge at the Ohio bank and 28 m at the Kentucky bank; the Cardinal pair leaves east south of W 8th. The CVG Express (16 October, Ben: its own span, and CVG Express New.kmz) leaves M and stays on the east side of the APEx approach, crossing only the Cardinal pair at grade south of W 8th (the Cardinal runs about 1.4 trains an hour each way); it crosses the Ohio on its own double-track span beside the APEx bridge, about 6 m clear of it (span ends from Ben’s Bridge Spans.kmz), then within 250 m of the Kentucky bank comes in to 15.75 m from the APEx centre (Ben, 17 October: the APEx’s outer track 6.75 m, the CVG Express’s inner track 13.5 m, group spacing) and goes through Ludlow and the hills beside the APEx in its own bores, level with it, then climbs 1.7% from about 3.5 km past the bank, crosses over the APEx (15 m, a working value for bore over bore) just north of Erlanger, where it turns west, and levels at 209.5 m, 40 m under the CS line. From there it is deep-bored (Ben, CVG Express Final.kmz): in its own bores under the RER B/E line to where the RER turns off north-west (E 707,285 N 4,323,668), under the creek valley east of the airport (floor about 222 m; 12 m rail to floor, a working value), then 0.76% up to 238.5 m and on under 18L/36R (762 m curve) to the terminals, an airside train with five stations (option 1): T3, T2 and T4 on a straight north-north-west, T4 under the shallow RER (working values: the RER about 12 m down, the CVG Express 15 m below it), then a 380 m curve onto the 1969 line west through T1B and T1A. The stations are at Ben’s stop points, 366, 266, 818 and 672 m apart and 21 to 29 m below the ground; T3 is 15.3 km and T1A 17.4 km from the south end of platform M. The platforms are 200 m (Ben: four 50 m or three 67 m units), side platforms with screen doors (GoA3) as drawn, centred on the stop points except at T1B (80 m west, so it clears the 380 m curve) and T1A (14 m east, clear of the curve to the yard). From the split to the yard the line is in the station editor like the RER and Metro: select a station to move its platforms along the line, make them island or side, or delete it; the line itself can be edited. Beyond T1A the line curves north (475 m) and comes up 2.0% to the yard and maintenance building north-west of T1A (portal about 880 m past T1A, under the tip of T1A’s west pier). The 1969 line (Original in the KMZ, drawn as a former line) came in from the south-east on the line the RER now uses to about E 705,000 and ran west across the north end of 18L to T1A; extending 18L north cut it, and the new Terminals 2 and 3 took the line south in the bore, which joins the old line just east of T1B. The intercity pair (A-D) has the CS bridge to itself. Eight tracks cross the river: the CS bridge (intercity 2), the APEx bridge (4) and the CVG span (2). In Ludlow (Ben: 4 APEx tracks, at least 2 intercity, 6 to 8 in all) the two CS tracks carry the intercity trains across the 1922 bridge and on at grade along the TRIMET CS line, with the APEx and the CVG Express east of them; the CS pair follows the CS line’s own Kentucky approach (Ben’s CS Bridge KY Approach.kmz), bending west off its bridge, and the APEx runs straight off its bridge on the KMZ bearing (204°) with no curve until the hills (Ben, 17 October). Drawn exactly on the KMZ line the APEx would pass 8.4 m from the CS line at N 4,330,200, where both are at about 161-165 m, so the APEx line (bridge and straight together) is drawn 7.5 m east of the KMZ line, the least that keeps 15.75 m to the CS pair. It goes 2% down from the end of its span into a tunnel about 360 m from it and stays in tunnel through the hills toward Erlanger, so it is grade-separated from the CS line throughout (Ben): 146 m where it passes under the CS line, about 1 km from the bank (22.6 m below), and under the valley at about N 4,329,000, then 1% up. RER E leaves the APEx tunnel corridor and passes over the APEx; the KMZ lines put the three on top of one another three times, each within 20 m of the CS line: ORR/intercity at grade on top, RER E between, the APEx below (RER E drawn midway, a working value). The CS pair carries only the intercity trains (4 an hour each way, a placeholder), so a spare pair is not needed yet; the band leaves room for one on the west side. E-H feed the C&O pair, which stays at -2 under W 6th and then climbs onto the TRIMET C&O viaduct.

TRI A passes under the lower level at or below 135.9 m (Ben: accepted); TRI B/D can share its level at the Union Terminal TRI box, or sit above it. Open: Lines 7, 8 and 10 not profiled; Line 4 between Garrard and Chinatown against the CS pair.

Deck base: Sanborn 1904 Cincinnati, vol. 1 sheets 33-35, 47, 57-58, 67-69, 77; vol. 3 sheets 201-202, 211-212, 221-222, 233-234, 245-246, 253-254; vol. 5 sheets 460 (upper panel), 461-464, 466-468, 491-496, 504-505, 511-514, 519-520, 522. Since 13 October the deck sheets are fitted by the basin method: the street crossings on every sheet, tied to the neighbouring sheets, to surviving OSM corners and to the 1912 city survey, in one adjustment with the north sheets (ties median 0.8 m, OSM 1.7 m, 1912 survey 2.1 m). The first deck fit had hung sheets 33-35 and 47 on very few points and placed them 13-20 m too far west (57 by 8-10 m); the 1922 atlas, the 1912 survey and the surviving piece of Horne Street agree on the new positions, and Garrard and Horne moved east with them. Lower Price Hill block (State Ave to Western Ave, Gest St to the river): the 1904 sheets are bent onto the 1922 atlas, vol. 1 plates 3, 6, 26 and 29, with a thin-plate spline through the crossings both atlases print (held within 0.3 m), easing back to the plain fit over the last 80 m before Gest and Western Ave; the creek seam between the vol. 5 and vol. 1 sheets is gone. One corner is left as fitted: at the creek mouth by W 6th, sheet 34 and 1922 plate 3 draw the creek bend about 35 m east of where the 1912 survey has it, with no crossing within 150 m to settle it. Deck streets that carry a West End street across the yards start where that street's basin centreline meets the deck edge and keep its line as far as the Garrard line. The Garrard line, fitted to the 1904 sheets, is moved 9 m west as a whole (13 October), as Garrard was never a set street across the yards: it now runs into Camp Washington's Garrard Avenue at Township (0.9 m off its line; the 1904 sheets put the avenue 8.7 to 9.4 m west of the old fit). There each bends, by up to about 1.2 degrees, onto the boulevard's square (93.64 degrees), so every street west of Garrard meets the boulevard at a right angle; the two grids meet along Garrard, as the downtown grids meet along Market Street in San Francisco. Gest bends onto the grid too and meets State Avenue 11 m north of its 1904 corner, since the upper part of Lower Price Hill is cleared for the Exposition. W 8th (the viaduct line) fronts surviving 1904 blocks, so it is straightened only part way, as a long-lived street would be: one straight run from Garrard to State on a bearing halfway between its old line and the grid (93.94 degrees), moving its south side 1.5 to 2.5 m and its corner at State 4.8 m south. Its crossings west of Garrard are 0.3 degrees off square, and Line 4 stays under it (at most 5.7 m off the centreline; State station 14.3 m, inside the 33.5 m street). North of Bank the deck streets continue Dolph, McRoberts and Western Avenue, and Harrison's re-aligned avenue meets Harrison at the deck edge. Dayton, Exeter and York stop at the outer wall on both sides of the lower pairs' open cut.

Plots (15 October; unfinished, tabled): the lot lines were drawn without a check against the sheets and many do not follow them (Ben: Pleasant Court, and the block of Smith, Central, 2nd and 3rd, are the worst; angled lots where nothing is angled, as on Dalton between Sherman and Findlay); they are to be redrawn. As they stand: every block is divided into plots, measured and snapped. On each block face that fronts a street the lot width is measured from the 1904 sheet (the repeat of its printed lot lines), the lots are cut square to the street at that width, and each cut moves onto the nearest printed lot line within 2.5 m; faces with no clear repeat use even widths. Corner lots front the longer face, and land deeper than the lots on very deep blocks is left as interior plots. Deck blocks are divided the same way at the density of the basin around them. The use is filled in only where the sheet says it plainly: 5,679 printed labels (church, school, hall, engine house, store, saloon, office, bank, garage, livery, factory, works, a named company, mill, foundry, brewery, warehouse and the like) were read off the sheets and placed on 4,227 plots; a plot with labels of more than one kind takes the commonest. Unlabelled plots, the dwellings among them, are not set, and blocks typed as worship, civic, school, hospital, industrial, square, park or planting lend their plots that use until a plot is set. The plots follow the streets as they are edited: a street moved, widened or lengthened cuts the plots under its new corridor, and the land its old corridor leaves goes to the plots that front it, out to the old centre line, so blocks stretch and join; land no plot fronts is left as open land. Plots can be split along a line, merged with a neighbour, divided into lots, and given a use and a name, one by one or a whole block at a time. Since 15 October several things can be selected at once (Shift, Ctrl or Cmd click, or a box dragged with one of them held): selected plots that make one piece merge into one plot, and street pieces merge into one street (the first selected keeps its name and width; Unmerge puts them back).
